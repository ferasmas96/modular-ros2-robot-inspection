Extrinsic derivation
- Measured approximate translations: base_link -> imu_0_link = +0.295 m Z;
  base_link -> helios_16p_link = +0.342 m Z; rotations identity.
- T_imu_lidar = inverse(T_base_imu) * T_base_lidar is identity rotation,
  approximately +0.047 m Z (direct reported sensor-to-sensor transform about
  +0.048 m). This expresses the LiDAR frame/origin in IMU coordinates.
- GLIM defines p_lidar = T_lidar_imu * p_imu; therefore GLIM needs the inverse:
  identity rotation and approximately -0.048 m Z, quaternion [0,0,0,1].
  Resolve the 0.001 m rounded-value difference from unrounded TF before runtime.
  This is the provisional simulated mount, not physical calibration.

IMU and timing modes
- Inspected IMU callback consumes linear_acceleration, angular_velocity, and
  message stamp; it ignores orientation and covariance. Keep SI units
  (acc_scale=1, ang_scale=1), verify the live values/stamp monotonicity, and
  keep the robot stationary through the CPU config's about-3-second
  initialization. Observed stationary +Z acceleration near 9.8 m/s^2 is
  consistent with SI specific force; simplified noise/zero covariance are not
  a hardware-quality sensor model.
- Mode A: points_glim, FLOAT32 "time" zero seconds; relative-time scale 1.0,
  global_shutter_lidar=true. This is an instantaneous approximation and disables
  deskewing.
- Mode B: original points topic without a time field; global_shutter_lidar=false.
  TimeKeeper estimates scan duration from cloud header intervals and assigns
  pseudo-times by point order. At 10 Hz this is approximately 0.1 s, not actual
  GPU-LiDAR firing timing; first scan has no prior interval. Test separately;
  no accuracy comparison has been made.
- Existing evidence: LiDAR about 10 Hz, IMU about 100 Hz, occasional multi-second
  scheduling gaps, EKF missed-update warnings, and high Gazebo CPU/RSS use.
  First runtime should use CPU modules, few threads, no RViz/viewer/map
  subscription, then a stationary startup before user-controlled movement.
